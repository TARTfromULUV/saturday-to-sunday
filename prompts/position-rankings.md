# The best players at every position

Ten quarterbacks, ten backs, ten corners: the site's own ranking of the
players at each position. In the preseason it is a season preview; in season
it is the ranking with this year's production beside last year's.

700 to 950 words.

## What you have

`positions` — one list per position (`QB`, `RB`, `WR`, `TE`, `OL`, `EDGE`,
`DT`, `LB`, `CB`, `S`), each with a `label` and up to ten `players` in this
site's order. Each player carries his `rank` in that list, `team` (and
`teamRank` when it is ranked), class `year`, what he was `recruitedAs`
out of high school, `arrived` when he is in his first season at the school
after a transfer, and two stat lines: `lastSeason` (with `at` naming the
school when it was a different one) and `thisSeason` (absent before the
opener). `basis` says which situation this is. `userTeam` is the
program this site covers; any of its players on a list get a clause.

The order is this site's. It is built on the players, not on the stat
lines, and the stat lines are what you argue it with. **Never cite a
rating, a grade, a number behind the order, or anything not in the
lists.**

## Structure

- Open on the best player in the country at any position, with the line
  that proves it.
- Then each position with a subhead. Not ten entries each — that is a
  hundred paragraphs. For each position: the No. 1 and the case, the one
  ranked higher than his production would suggest (and why the site has him
  there: class, pedigree, what he did somewhere else), and the name at the
  bottom of the list who will be at the top of it next year. Mention the
  rest in a line.
- Where a position's top is close, say what separates one from two.
- Close on the position where the country is deepest this year and the one
  where it is thin.

## Rules

- Production comes from `lastSeason` and `thisSeason` only. A player with
  neither (a freshman, a lineman) is argued from his stars (`recruitedAs`), his class and
  his place on the list — never from an invented stat, snap count or start.
- Linemen have no stat line and that is normal; never print a lineman's
  zeroes or say he "has no statistics".
- No draft stock, no round grades: that is the Draft desk's job.
- Never compare anyone to a real professional player.
- No quotes.

## Treatments

- Position by position: the default, as above.
- The All-America team: build a first team and a second team from the
  lists (one quarterback, two backs, three receivers, one tight end, five
  linemen, four defensive linemen, three linebackers, four defensive backs)
  and argue the three or four spots where the second-teamer has the better
  numbers.
- The freshmen and the transfers: the players on any list who are true
  freshmen or who `arrived` this year, position by position, and what it
  says about how rosters are built now. Everyone else in a line each.
- Ten names: pick the ten best players in the country regardless of
  position from across the lists, order them, and argue the order — the
  quarterback against the edge rusher, the corner against the back.
- (requires: userTeam) The covered program's players: every one of its
  players on any list, where he sits and against whom, and the positions
  where it has nobody in the top ten. Then the national No. 1 at each
  position in a line.
