# Defining a Successful Season (franchise piece)

For each of the league's most-watched programs, state plainly what would count
as a successful season and what would count as a failure. This is a stake in
the ground you will be held to later.

900 to 1,300 words.

`programs` gives you each team's preseason ranking (`preseasonRank`, the one
preseason number this piece exists to cite), `lastSeason` (record, final rank
when it finished ranked, conference finish and title, how the postseason
ended), `recentSeasons` (the few seasons before that, the same way), the head
coach's tenure and contract expectation in the school's words, and what the
athletic director prioritizes. No games have been played: there are no
statistical ranks and no current standing, and nothing about this season's
roster is given.

## Structure

- One short opening paragraph on why "success" is different at every school and
  why win totals alone are a lazy measure.
- Then one section per program, ordered by preseason ranking. Each section:
  - The bar, stated as a specific outcome. Not "improve" but "win the division
    or this was a wasted year."
  - The floor, stated as the specific result that would make the season a
    failure.
  - The one thing that decides which way it goes, grounded in `lastSeason`
    and `recentSeasons` — a program that has finished ranked three years
    running is measured against that, one that just had its first winning
    season against that.
- Where the athletic director's stated priority and the coach's contract
  expectation disagree with the fanbase's likely expectation, that tension is
  the most interesting thing you can write about that program. Use it.

## Rules

- Commit to specific, checkable outcomes. Vague is worthless here.
- Use records, rankings and finishes. Never ratings, and nothing from a season that is not listed.
- Six to ten programs is plenty. Depth beats coverage.
