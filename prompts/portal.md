# The portal

Roster churn week. You are given `departedLeaders` — the players from last
season's national statistical leaderboards who are no longer on their
rosters, with the production that made them matter: the stat named (e.g.
`rushingYards`), `games`, `perGame`, `nationalRank` on that leaderboard, and
`detail` for the rest of the line — and `holesByContender`, the same losses
grouped by the teams that finished ranked (one program is included on
readership rather than rank; it gets the same treatment as the others).
`openNow` says which windows the calendar has open right now. `justOpened`
means the portal window opened this week, which is the news.

500 to 750 words.

## The one rule that is not negotiable here

A name on `departedLeaders` means OFF THE ROSTER, and that is all it means. A departed senior
probably graduated; a departed underclassman may have transferred or turned
pro; the data does not say, so neither do you. Never state which door an
individual player left through — no "entered the portal", no "declared", no
invented destination school, no rumored landing spots. What you may write is
the fact of the loss and the size of the hole: who is gone, what he produced,
what his team now has to replace. Totals are yours ("three of the top ten
passers are gone"); mechanisms are not.

## The shape

Open on the biggest hole in the sport — the most production walking out of one
building — not on a generality about the portal era. Then work through the
contenders' losses using `holesByContender`, biggest hole first. A program
gets a section because of the size of what it lost, not because of who is
reading.

If `classRanks` is present, close on the other half of the ledger: who is
reloading through recruiting, and whose class rank does not look big enough
for what just walked out. If it is absent, close on the contender with the
most to replace and do not mention recruiting classes at all.

## Rules

- Production numbers, games and per-game figures are published facts and are
  yours to use. One well-chosen figure per player beats a stat line dump.
- A team's final ranking may be stated once, as "finished the season ranked
  No. N". Nothing rises or falls in the offseason.
- Do not invent replacements, incoming transfers or names not in what you are given.
  What you are given knows who LEFT; it does not know who arrives.
- Position scarcity is a real angle: if every departed leader at one position
  clusters, say so.

## Treatments

- The exodus ledger: the default — the biggest holes in the sport, contender
  by contender, then the reload if class data is present.
- Position of need: organize by position instead of by team. Which position
  lost the most production league-wide, which contenders share the same hole,
  and who is suddenly shopping in the same aisle.
- The hardest-hit program: lead almost entirely with the single contender
  that lost the most — every departure, what each one produced, the honest
  size of the rebuild — and use the rest of the churn only as context for how
  everyone else is bleeding too.
- Winners and losers of the churn: sort the contenders by how much they lost
  — who lost least and who got hollowed out. You know only who left, never
  who stayed, so "kept its core" is never written; "lost nothing from the
  leaderboards" is. The argument is which finished-ranked team's offseason
  has quietly changed its ceiling.
