# The offseason column

The flagship, in the months with no football. Same panel format as the season
column — the masthead's writers each take a question and sign their answer —
but the subject is the offseason itself: the windows that are open right now
(`openNow`), the players who are gone (`departedLeaders`), the classes coming
in (`classRanks`, when present), and the draft board (`draftBoard`).

600 to 850 words.

## The rules that make this piece work

- **The championship is over and covered.** This site wrote it up when it
  happened. It gets at most one clause of passing reference in the whole
  piece — never a paragraph, never its statistics, never "what it exposed."
  A column that re-litigates the title game in March is the exact failure
  this template exists to prevent.
- **A `departedLeaders` entry means off the roster and nothing more.** Graduated, drafted,
  transferred — the data does not say, so neither do you. Total the losses,
  weigh the holes, and never assert which door an individual left through or
  where he is going.
- **No poll movement, no new top ten.** The brief already says it; it bears
  repeating because this is the column.
- People come from what you are given, as always. No invented arrivals, no rumored
  commitments, no unnamed sources.

## The open

Two or three sentences on where the offseason actually stands — week and
stage are in the brief, and `openNow` says what is live this week. The open
names the single most consequential thing happening right now, then hands off
to the panel.

## The questions

Three or four `##` sections, each a question one writer answers and signs,
the way the season column works. Build the questions from what the data can
actually answer this week. Good question shapes, matched to their data:

- Which contender lost the most, and can what is coming replace it?
  (`departedLeaders` against `classRanks`.)
- Who is the best player in the sport right now, with last season's top
  producers gone? (`departedLeaders` for who left, `draftBoard` for who is
  climbing toward the next draft.)
- Which program has the most to prove by September? (Argued from
  `departedLeaders` and `classRanks` — who lost the most, who reloaded least.)
- What does this week's open window actually decide? (`openNow` — a signing
  window, portal activity, staff hiring — and its stakes for the teams
  named in the data.)

Do not use all of these every week. Pick the ones this week's data makes
sharp, and let the treatment set the piece's centre of gravity.

## The end

End on the last question, or a closing line on the offseason's biggest open
question. No ranking, no wrap-up paragraph, no preview of a season that has no
schedule yet.

## Treatments

- (requires: departedLeaders) The league checkup: the panel works the
  national questions — biggest losses, the reload gap between the top
  classes and the top departures, the one program whose offseason is
  quietly going best.
- (requires: departedLeaders) The rebuild audit: most of the piece is the
  one contender facing the biggest rebuild — its losses one by one, what its
  class rank actually buys — with the national picture as backdrop. No coach
  is given here; nothing about anyone's history with rebuilds.
- (requires: openNow) The calendar piece: built around `openNow`. What this
  window is, who it matters to most, and what will be settled when it closes
  that is not settled today.
- (requires: departedLeaders) The names: pick four or five players who
  define this offseason — a departed star whose hole reshapes a contender, a
  draft-board riser — and give each a section of his own. Players only; no
  coach appears in this slice.
- (requires: classRanks) The September test: pick three or four programs,
  name the one thing each must fix before kickoff from what it lost and what
  its class rank says it added, and say how the offseason's remaining windows
  can or cannot fix it.
- The ledger: the panel's five questions as a plain accounting of the
  offseason so far — what is open, what has closed, what the board and the
  class table say right now — answered briefly, with the top ten at the end.
