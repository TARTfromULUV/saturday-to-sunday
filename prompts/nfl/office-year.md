# The front office's year

The offseason column on the club's business year: what the owner did
with the money, how the season's calls turned out, and what rivals did. A
business columnist's year-end piece, measured and specific.

## What you are given

- `team`: the club.
- `report`: the year (`year`), `moneyIn` and `moneyOut` (millions of
  dollars, the year's deals, fees, royalties and bonuses against its
  projects, campaigns and buyouts), the calls (`signed`, `approved`,
  `passed`, `lost` to rivals), `best` (the biggest deal signed: `title`,
  `perYear`, `years`) and `away` (the biggest deal a rival took: `title`,
  `rival`, `perYear`). 
- `score`: a grade from -2 to 5 the app gives the year; use it only to set
  the tone (5 a banner year, below 0 a hard one). Never print it.
- `rivals`: what rival clubs did this year (`title`; `fromYou` when it
  was a deal this club let go).
- `stand`: where the club ranks in `group` against `of` clubs
  (`rows`: `label`, `rank`). These ranks are from the season itself.

## The shape

350 to 550 words. A headline that names the club and the year's story.

**1. The year in one paragraph:** money in against money out, said plainly.
**2. The calls that mattered:** the best one, and the one that got away.
**3. Around the division:** two or three rival moves, and where the club
ranks.
**4. What comes next:** one paragraph on what the owner has to answer for
next season. Measured.

## Rules

- Money exactly as given, in millions ("$2.1 million"; "$480,000 a year"
  under a million).
- Never say how the business year changed wins, recruiting or attendance.
- The owner is "the owner" or "ownership". Never name them. No quotes.
- A brand is a partner like any other: report it, never sell it.
- Never say the game is a game, a save or a file. It is the season.
