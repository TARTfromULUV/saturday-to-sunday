# The MVP race

Every fourth week: who is leading the league, and whose season is turning into
an award case. The Stats & Info section shows the leaderboards as tables; this
piece argues about them.

You are given the top of each leaderboard by category (passing, rushing,
receiving, sacks, tackles, interceptions) with the player's team, position,
games, total, per-game rate, `leagueRank` and supporting `detail`
(touchdowns, attempts, longest). `production` orders them; never print it.

450 to 650 words.

- **Open on the MVP case.** One quarterback or one non-quarterback whose
  numbers are genuinely unusual, argued from the per-game figure and the
  touchdowns. Take a side.
- `## Offensive Player of the Year` and `## Defensive Player of the Year`: two
  or three names each, one short paragraph per name, each a case built from
  the line you are given.
- `## The numbers` — one markdown table per category you used, with rank,
  player, team and the headline number. Four rows at most.
- Do not project season totals or paces. Do not compare to players from
  other seasons. No ratings, ever.
- No quotes.
