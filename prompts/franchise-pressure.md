# Coaching Pressure Index (franchise piece)

A ranked list of the head coaches under the most pressure, and the case for each.

You are NOT given a job security number and must not invent one. You get the
evidence: record, conference standing, current rank or `fellOutOfPoll`,
streak, contract years remaining and stated expectation, the athletic
director's demeanor and priority, last week's result, the program's history
and `recentSeasons`, and the school's own reading of the seat in August and
now (`standingAtSeasonStart`, `standingNow`).

Three lists arrive. **`underPressure` is the index** — the ranked list this
piece is. `worsenedThisWeek` is who slid this week, for the open. `onTheRise`
is the other side of the market, coaches winning at smaller jobs: material for
the close only, never entries in the index.

700 to 1,000 words.

## Structure

- Open with a paragraph on what actually gets a coach fired, using this
  season's evidence. It is rarely the record alone. It is the gap between what
  the program expected and what it is getting.
- Then the list, most pressure first. Each entry: the coach, the school, and a
  real paragraph making the case. Cite the record, the standing, whether the
  team fell out of the poll, the contract situation, the AD's stated priority,
  and where the program was before him (`recentSeasons`). Never a preseason
  rank.
- Distinguish kinds of pressure. A coach in year two with a patient AD and four
  years left is not in the same position as a coach whose contract asks for a
  division title and who is sitting eighth in the league.
- Close on one coach people assume is in trouble who you think is safe, and say
  why — or, if `onTheRise` has names, on the man a school in trouble would
  call.

## Rules

- Never say "index", "job security", "percentage" or "rating" in the prose,
  despite the title of the piece.
- Never report a firing as decided.
- These are people's jobs. Be direct, not gleeful.
- Prose and a numbered list. No tables.
