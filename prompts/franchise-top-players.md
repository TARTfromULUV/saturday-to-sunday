# Top 10 Players (franchise piece)

A ranked list of the ten best players in the country right now, built from
production. `leaders` gives you top tens by passing, rushing, receiving, sacks,
tackles and interceptions. Each row names the stat (`passingYards`, `sacks`…),
`games`, `perGame`, `nationalRank` on that leaderboard, `teamRank` when his
team is ranked, `year`, and `detail` with the rest of the line (touchdowns,
attempts, completions, tackles for loss…).

`mostProductive.offense` and `mostProductive.defense` are the same players
in the site's own production order (`productionRank`), which counts
touchdowns with the yards and sacks, tackles for loss, interceptions and
forced fumbles with the tackles. Start your ten from those two lists; the
single-stat top tens are for the evidence.

700 to 1,000 words.

## How to rank

- Production first: touchdowns and takeaways count, not yards alone. A back
  with 600 yards and nine touchdowns has outproduced one with 700 and two.
- Weight quality of position. A defensive player who leads the country in sacks
  belongs above a receiver with ordinary numbers on a good team.
- Weight context. A back putting up huge yardage for an unranked team is doing
  something different from one behind the best line in the sport. `teamRank`
  tells you which is which.
- Efficiency matters, not only totals. Touchdowns against interceptions,
  completions against attempts, yards per game against games played.

## Structure

- Open on the one player whose numbers are genuinely absurd, with the per-game
  figure that proves it.
- The ten, numbered, each with the stat line that earns the spot and one line of
  argument.
- A short "just missed" section naming three more.
- If a freshman or a player on an unranked team is in the top ten, make that a
  point rather than a footnote.

## Rules

- Never cite an overall rating or any attribute number. Production only.
- Do not compare anyone to a real-world NFL player.
- Do not project season totals.

## Treatments

- The list: the default — the ten, ranked and argued from production.
- The trophy race: rank the ten as the award race — if the season ended
  today, who takes the sport's biggest individual trophy, who are the
  finalists, and what separates the top three. Same players, same published
  numbers, argued as a horse race instead of a list.
- The units: organize by position group — the best quarterback season, the
  best back, the best receiver, the best defender — and crown one player
  per unit before naming the overall one.
- The underclassmen: the same ten, but the piece is organised around `year`
  — which of the ten are freshmen and sophomores, what that says about next
  season, and which seniors are producing their last.
