# Realignment

The Encourage Transfers week is when the conference map can change for next
season, and this piece is the desk's proposal for how it should. It is an
opinion column with a plan: specific schools moving to specific
conferences, argued from the season's facts.

600 to 900 words.

## What you are given

- `nextSeason`: the season the new map would take effect.
- `arc`: the direction the reader chose, or null for the desk's own call.
  - `status-quo`: argue the map should hold, and name the one or two
    tweaks at the margins worth making.
  - `power`: consolidation: the strongest programs concentrated into the
    strongest conferences.
  - `parity`: spread the power: strong programs moved where they lift a weak
    conference, so more leagues have a real contender.
  - `regional`: geography and rivalries: put rivals back in the same
    conference and shorten the map.
  - null: decide which of these this year's facts argue for, and say why.
- `conferences`: each conference, its members with this season's record,
  final rank when ranked, and donor money in millions when known; and how
  many of its members finished ranked.
- `rivalries`: the named rivalries and their two schools.
- `userTeam`: the covered program.

## Structure

1. **The case**: the state of the map in two or three sentences, and the
   direction (the `arc`, or the one you chose).
2. `## The moves`: three to six specific moves, a `###` each ("### Boise
   State to the Big 12"): the school, where it goes, and the argument from
   the facts given (records, ranks, money, rivalries).
3. `## What it does to the map`: the conferences after the moves, in a short
   list: who gains, who loses.
4. `## For us`: what it means for the covered program.

## Rules

- It is a proposal, and reads as one: "should", "the case for". Nothing has
  been decided, announced or voted on; no officials, no quotes, no sources.
- Every record, rank and figure comes from what you are given.
- Never mention the game, a save, a setting or a tool. The `arc` is the
  desk's argument, not a setting the reader picked.
