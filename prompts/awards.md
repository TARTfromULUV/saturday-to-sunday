# Awards night

The season's hardware is out. `winners` is every award the sport just handed
out — the Heisman, the positional awards, the players of the year — and
`allAmericans` is the All-America teams, one list per team (first team,
second team, freshman team). `leaders` is the top five of each national
statistical leaderboard. This is the piece that reads the envelope.

550 to 800 words.

## The data, and the honesty rules

- Every entry in `winners` carries the award (`honor`), the player, position
  and school, a `seasonLine` with his season statistics, `nationalRanks`
  where he sits on a national leaderboard, and `teamNow` with his team's
  record and rank.
- **Some awards list MORE THAN ONE name.** The save does not say which of
  them won. Write those names as the honorees or finalists for that award —
  never crown one of them unless the award lists exactly one player.
- **The same player under several awards is one story, not five.** Say he
  swept them, once, and spend the words elsewhere.
- The `seasonLine` is the ONLY source for a player's numbers. A winner with
  no seasonLine (a lineman, a specialist) gets the case made from his team's
  season and the award itself, never from invented statistics.
- Never invent vote totals, margins, ceremonies or speeches. A snub argument
  may only name players who appear in `leaders` or the All-America lists,
  and must argue from their listed numbers against the winner's.
- If `followUp` is true, the reveal ran LAST issue (see the recent coverage
  list). This piece is the second look — the argument, not the announcement.
  Do not re-announce the winners as news.

## The shape

Open on the biggest award — one paragraph that treats the winner's season as
the argument the voters accepted. Then work down the card: group the awards
sensibly (the big three, offense, defense, specialists), a short paragraph or
a tight two-sentence entry each, every claim pointed at the seasonLine. Close
on the All-America teams as a readout — the roll call is the texture, pick
out two or three names worth a clause and let the rest stand as a list.

No quotes. Nobody spoke.

## Treatments

- The envelope: the default — the card read top to bottom, winner by winner,
  the case for each made from their line.
- The case for the winner: the whole piece argues the biggest award — why
  this season won it, measured against the other statistical seasons on the
  leaderboards. The rest of the card gets a readout section at the end.
- The snubs: lead with who DIDN'T win — leaderboard seasons that came up
  empty-handed, argued from the numbers — then concede what the winners had
  that they did not. Names only from what you are given.
- The team behind the trophy: anchor on what the awards say about the teams —
  which programs stacked hardware, which conferences, who won awards from
  losing teams (`teamNow`).
- The freshman line: build around the youngest names on the card and the
  All-America freshman team — the sport's next three years, read off one
  night's results.
