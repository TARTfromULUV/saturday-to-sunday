# Programs on the rise

The story underneath the coaching desk. Not who is in trouble and not who
moved: which programs are becoming something they were not, and which are
quietly becoming less. Once a season.

600 to 850 words.

## What you have

`basis` says what the comparison is: this season against the last three
completed ones, or the season just finished against the three before it.

`risers` — the programs whose results have climbed furthest from their own
recent past, biggest climb first. `fallers` — the ones that have slid
furthest, weighted toward the big jobs, where a slide is a story. Each
carries:

- `thisSeason` or `lastSeason` — the record the comparison is built on, with
  `conferenceRecord` and `conferenceStanding` during the season.
- `rank` — the current media-poll rank, when ranked. Absent means unranked;
  say "unranked" only when that is the point.
- `headCoach` — who has the job, which season of his tenure this is
  (`currentSeasonAtSchool`), his years in coaching, his `specialty`.
- `programStanding` — where the job ranks among the ~140 in the country by
  what the program has ever won. A low number is a big job.
- `program` — the all-time history, and `recentSeasons`: the last few
  completed seasons, newest first, each with the record, the conference
  finish, a conference title when there was one, the final poll rank when it
  finished ranked, how the postseason ended, and the coach who had the job.
  **This list is the whole argument.** Every "three years ago they were",
  every "has not finished ranked since", every "the third straight" is read
  off it. A season not on the list did not happen as far as you know.

## Structure

- **Open on the riser that means the most**: the biggest climb from the
  lowest base, or the one happening at a school whose history says it should
  not be. State the arc in records — "4-8, 6-6, 7-5, and now 9-1" — before
  you say anything else about it. That sentence is the piece.
- **The risers, one at a time.** For each: the arc in records, what changed
  in the years listed (a new coach appears in `recentSeasons` the year he
  arrived; a conference title; a first ranked finish), and what this season's
  numbers say about whether it holds (`conferenceStanding`, the rank, the
  offense and defense ranks when the program card carries them). Where the
  coach arrived and the climb started the same year, say so; where the climb
  predates him, say that instead.
- **The fallers.** Shorter, and direct. The arc in records, the year it
  turned, and whether the man in charge is the one who built what is being
  lost or the one who inherited it. These are people's jobs: no gloating, no
  predictions of a firing.
- **Close on the one program whose next season decides which list it is on.**

## Rules

- Every record, rank, finish and title comes from what you are given. Never
  cite a season that is not in `recentSeasons`, and never say what a program
  was "historically" beyond the all-time counters in `program`.
- A coach named on an earlier season held the job that year. You do not know
  why he left; never say.
- No recruiting classes, no transfers, no facilities, no money. None of it is
  given.
- No quotes.
- Never call a rise "sustainable" or a fall "terminal". Say what the records
  say and stop.
- Headline makes a claim about one program's arc — never a label like
  "Programs on the rise".

## Treatments

- The climbers: the default — the risers in full, the fallers briefly, the
  close on the program whose next season decides it.
- The turn: organise every entry around the one season where its arc turned,
  from `recentSeasons`. What was true the year before, what has been true
  since, who was coaching on each side of the line. Risers and fallers
  interleaved by how sharp the turn was.
- (requires: fallers) The slide: lead with the fallers — the big programs
  going the wrong way, each against its own history — and run the risers as
  the second half, as the programs that are taking what the fallers are
  losing in their own leagues.
- The coaches: write it as five or six tenures. For each riser whose coach
  arrived at or just before the climb, the job as he found it
  (`recentSeasons` before his first year) and the job as it is now. Fallers
  as the counter-examples. Everything stated in records; nothing about why
  anyone was hired or fired.
