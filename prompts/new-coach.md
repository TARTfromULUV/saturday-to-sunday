# Year one

How last offseason's hires are actually going, at the jobs where it matters.

400 to 600 words. The carousel piece asked who moved. This one asks whether the
school got it right, and it is only worth writing about programs with something
to lose.

## What you have

`coaches` — first-year head coaches, biggest job first. Each carries:

- `programStanding` — where this job ranks among the ~140 in the country by
  what the program has won. A low number is a blue blood.
- `program` — this season's record, conference record and standing, current
  rank if they have one, national offensive and defensive ranks, current
  streak, the program's all-time history, and `recentSeasons`: the last few
  seasons before this one, each with the record, the final rank when it had
  one, the conference finish and the coach who had the job. That is what he
  inherited, in numbers.
- `careerRecordBeforeAndIncludingThisSeason`, `careerVsRanked` — his career
  record and his career record against ranked teams, every job included.
  This season's record at this school is `program.record` and nothing else.
- `almaMater`, `specialty`, `offensiveScheme`, `defensiveScheme`,
  `yearsCoaching` — who he is.
- `contractYearsRemaining`, `contractExpectation` — what he was hired to do,
  in the school's own words.

## Structure

- **Lead with the one that is going worst at the biggest job.** That is the
  story every year. Use the program's history and `recentSeasons` to establish
  the gap between what the school expects and what it is getting.
- **Then the one that is going best**, with the same specificity. A first-year
  coach with a top-25 defense somewhere that has never had one is a real story,
  and `recentSeasons` says whether he inherited it or built it.
- **Two or three more in a paragraph each.** For each, find the number that
  actually describes the season: the defensive rank, the conference standing,
  the streak, how it compares with the season before him.
- **Close on the hire the rest of the year will define**: the one whose
  verdict is still open, and what it rests on. No schedule is given here, so
  no named game to come.

## Judgement

Be honest and be early. It is one season; say so where it matters, but do not
hide behind it. A blue blood at 3-5 in year one is a bad start whatever the
excuse, and a writer who will not say that is not worth reading.

`contractExpectation` is what the school told him it wanted. Measuring the
season against that, rather than against your own standard, is the fairest
version of this piece.

## Rules

- Do not mention a job-security number, a percentage, or a hot seat rating.
  None of those exist. If a coach is in trouble, the record says so.
- No quotes from anyone.
- Do not speculate about whether a coach will be fired. Describe the season and
  what the school expected, and let the reader draw it.
- One season is one season. Do not write about a coach's future recruiting
  classes or roster plans; you have no information about either.
- Where he coached before is not given. Never name a previous job or say what
  it cost the school that lost him.

## Treatments

- Report cards: the default — each hire in turn, the situation he inherited
  (`recentSeasons`), what the season says so far.
- The expectation ledger: order by the gap between what each program's
  athletic department expected (`contractExpectation`) and what the record
  shows. The piece is about who is ahead of the deal they signed and who is
  already behind it.
- The inheritance: for each hire, set this season's record and ranks beside
  the last season under the previous coach, from `recentSeasons`. Who took a
  step, who held, who went backwards — and whether the one going backwards
  inherited a roster that was already falling.
- Scheme or roster: for each hire, one question — is the record his coaching
  or the roster he walked into? Argue it from the offense and defense ranks,
  his `specialty` and schemes, and the program's `recentSeasons`, and say
  which answers are still open.
