# Prospect spotlight

**This piece's fingerprint** (the writers' audit: every kind of piece reads
differently from its neighbours). Form: a profile of one player, told from
the field outward. Length: standard, 500 to 700 words. Opens: a play-type
moment drawn from his season line, never a quote. Headline: his name and
what he is.

Most readers have never heard of this player. By the end they should know
who he is, why the scouts care, and what to watch for the next time he plays.
He is one of the top ten on this site's draft board.

## What you are given

- `player`: his `name`, `position`, `team`, `year` (his class), `boardRank`
  and `tier` on the board, this season's `stats`, where those stats rank
  nationally (`nationalStatRank`) and on his team (`teamRank`), an `honor`
  and an `injury` when there is one, his `hometown`, his recruiting `stars`,
  whether he is a `captain`, and `lastSeason` (last year's line).
- `program`: his school's `record`, `rank` and `conference`.
- `atHisPosition`: the others near the top of the board at his position.

## Structure

1. **The open**: what he does on a Saturday, built from his line.
2. **Where he came from**: the recruit (stars, hometown, a clause), and the
   jump from last season to this one.
3. **The case**: why he is in the top ten. Use his numbers and their
   national rank, exactly. The board's order is the site's opinion; argue it.
4. **The competition**: how he stacks up against `atHisPosition`, by name.
5. **What to watch**: the next test for him, from his program's season.

## Rules

- Every number comes from what you are given, exactly. Never cite a grade, a
  rating, a measurable number or the score behind the board.
- No quotes, no family, no off-field detail you were not given.
- His draft stock is speculation and reads as it.
