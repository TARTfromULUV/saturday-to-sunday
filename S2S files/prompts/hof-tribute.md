# A Hall of Fame tribute

One inductee, one piece: the career told whole, the way a paper marks a
player going into the Hall. The class piece names everyone; this one is his.

600 to 900 words.

## What you are given

- `year`: the class year. `hall`: the hall he entered: `college`, `nfl`, or
  `universe` (both games: recruit to legend).
- `inductee`: his `name`, `position`, `college`, recruiting `stars` and
  `recruit` (national and position rank, the class year), `draft` (year,
  round, pick) when drafted, `team` (his last NFL club), `retired`,
  `collegeSeasons` and `proSeasons` (one line a season: the year, the team and
  his numbers), `collegeTotals` and `proTotals`, his college `honors`, NFL
  `proAwards` and `proBowls`, `reasons` (what put him over the bar),
  `labels` (the ledger's word on him: a steal, a bust, a blue-chip hit), and
  `callingCards`: the three things he did best, in words.

## Structure

1. **The open**: the moment that defined him, built from a real season in
   his lines (his best year, the honour that capped it). One paragraph.
2. **Where he came from**: the recruit (his stars, his rank, the class), the
   school, what was expected of him and whether he beat it (`labels`).
3. **The career**: season by season in prose, not a table. Pick the two or
   three seasons that tell it and give their numbers exactly. His calling
   cards are how he played; use them as description ("a corner who lived in
   man coverage"), never as ratings.
4. **The honours**: what the halls are recognising, from `reasons`,
   `honors`, `proAwards` and `proBowls`.
5. **For a universe inductee**, both halves: Saturday and Sunday, and the
   draft that joined them.
6. **The close**: one line on where he stands among those who came before.

## Rules

- Every figure, award, team and year comes from what you are given, exactly.
  No ratings, no overall, no attribute numbers.
- He is a real person to the reader. Write him as one: how he played, what
  the numbers meant, without inventing quotes, family, hometown details or
  anything that happened off the field.
- A retired player is retired: never call him a free agent or say he is
  still playing.
- No ceremony, speeches or dates you were not given.
