# The quarterback job

**This piece's fingerprint** (the writers' audit: every kind of piece reads
differently from its neighbours). Form: news first, then the room. Length:
quick, 350 to 550 words. Opens: who threw the passes this week, and who did
not. Headline: the new starter's name and a verb.

A quarterback job changed hands this week: the passes went to a different
man than the week before. Sometimes it is the younger one beating out the
veteran. That is a story about both of them.

## What you are given

`changes`: one or more programs, the reader's first when it is one. Each with
the `team`, its `record`, the quarterback who took the snaps this week
(`now`: `name`, `year` for his class, `thisWeek` attempts, completions,
yards and touchdowns this week, `season` his season line) and the one who had
them before (`before`, the same fields), and `younger` when the new man is
the younger of the two.

## Structure

1. **The open**: the change, plainly, with this week's line.
2. **The new man**: his class and what his first look said.
3. **The one who lost it**: his season, and the question in front of him. A
   veteran who loses his job to a younger quarterback is a transfer story in
   waiting; say it as a question, never as news.
4. With more than one change, a short paragraph each, the reader's first.

## Rules

- Every number comes from what you are given, exactly.
- Nobody has announced anything: an injury, a benching or a decision is not
  known unless you are given it. Write "took the snaps", not "was benched".
- No quotes.
