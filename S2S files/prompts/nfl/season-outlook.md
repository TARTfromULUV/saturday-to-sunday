# The season outlook

The flagship, the week the regular season begins. Cutdown day is done, every
club is at 53, and Week 1 is about to kick off. Same panel format as the
season column: the masthead's writers each take a question and sign their
answer. The subject is the whole season ahead, argued from `powerRankings`
(this site's opening ranking of all 32), `divisions` (every division, its
four clubs with their power rank and, where the save has it, `lastSeason`)
and `yourTeam` (the reader's club).

600 to 850 words.

## Nothing has been played

- **Every team is 0-0.** No regular-season game has happened. Never write that
  anyone opened, won, lost, or is "off to" anything. Preseason games do not
  count and are not mentioned.
- **No clinching, no paths.** Nobody controls its own fate, has a path to a
  division title, or needs to win out. Those are December words.
- Last season exists only as `lastSeason` gives it: a record, and whether the
  club made the playoffs, won its division, its conference or the Super Bowl.
  Nothing else about last season.
- People come from what you are given. No invented quotes, sources, or
  depth-chart battles. No quotes at all. A starter is named only from
  `yourTeam.starters` (the game's own depth chart, first at each spot), and
  a rookie only from `yourTeam.rookiesWhoMadeIt`.

`alsoInThisIssue` lists the other pieces in this issue (cutdown's results,
the best free agents left, the picks): do not retell them. `recentHeadlines`
are the last columns' headlines: do not reuse their angle or wording.

## The open

Three or four sentences, unsigned: the shape of the league going into Week 1,
from the top of `powerRankings`.

## The questions

Four `##` subheads, each a question, each answered and signed by one writer
from `THIS SITE'S WRITERS`, 130 to 200 words each:

1. **The league**: who is the team to beat, who is ranked too high, and the
   sleeper outside the top ten. From `powerRankings` and `lastSeason`.
2. **The AFC's divisions**: one line on each of the four, then the closest
   race and the one the rankings get wrong. From `divisions`.
3. **The NFC's divisions**: the same.
4. **The reader's club** (`yourTeam`): where it is ranked and whether that is
   right, what its division asks of it (`divisionRivals`), who starts
   (`starters`), the rookies who made the 53, and its opener (`opener`, the
   opponent and whether it is at home). End on what a good season looks like.

Without `yourTeam`, make the fourth question the season's biggest story line
from the rankings instead.

Never invent a player, a signing or a coaching change.

End on the last answer. No top ten this week; omit `analystTop10`.
