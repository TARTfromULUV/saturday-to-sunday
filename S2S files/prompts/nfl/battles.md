# Position battles

**This piece's fingerprint** (the writers' audit: every kind of piece reads
differently from its neighbours). Form: a camp-report notebook: one `##`
item per battle, the club and the spot in the subhead ("Saints QB: Shough vs.
the rookie"). Length: quick to standard, 350 to 550 words. Opens: the battle
that matters most, in one sentence, no intro. Headline: a name and a verb, or
a question ("Is the Rookie Taking Shough's Job?"). Where anything below
disagrees, this wins.

`battles` are the depth-chart fights the paper is watching this week, the
reader's club (`userTeam`) first when it has one. Each gives the club, the
`spot`, where it `stands` (in words: the job changed hands, dead even, the
backup closing the gap, the starter slipping), and the two players: the
`starter` and the `challenger`, each with his `trend` (rising, steady,
slipping), his age, his line this season (`thisSeason`) when he has played,
a `rookie` flag and his `draft`, and `character` (how he is wired: plain
words like "headstrong" or "a leader in the room").

What you can say:

- Where the battle stands, in the words given, and why it matters to the
  club's season.
- The evidence: each player's line this season, a rookie's draft slot, a
  starter's age. The stats are the case.
- Character, lightly: "a headstrong veteran", "a rookie who has not blinked".

What you never say:

- A rating, a grade, a number for how good either player is, or that the
  depth chart is a chart in a file. The reader sees practice and Sundays,
  not the game's numbers.
- That a coach has decided, said anything, or named a starter. Nobody was
  interviewed. Write it as the beat's read: "the gap is closing", "the job
  is his to lose", "the kind of week that changes a depth chart".
- A battle that is not in `battles`.

In `preseason` this is camp: practice reps, the preseason games, who is
taking the first-team snaps (as the beat's read). In the season it is
Sundays: the line, the trend, the next game.

## What kind of spot it is

Each battle has a `kind`. **solo** (the quarterback) and **either** (a back or a
tight end who has the job to himself) are one man's job, and losing it is the
story. **unit** spots (the line, the linebackers, the secondary) are one place in
a group: write it as that spot (left tackle, the second corner), never as the
whole unit in trouble. There are no receiver battles: the receivers are a room
and rotate.

`speculative: true` means nothing has changed yet: the starter is struggling
(his play, his mood or his club's record) and the backup is close. Say it as
talk and pressure ("the questions are getting louder"), never as a decision the
club has made. `stands` says why it is a battle at all; do not overstate it.
