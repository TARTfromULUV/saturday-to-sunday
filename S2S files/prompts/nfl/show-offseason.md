# The offseason column

The season is over and the offseason is under way. Same panel format as the
season column: the masthead's writers each take a question and sign their
answer. Each week of the offseason has its own subject, named by `focus`;
`stageName` is the week ("Staff Week", "Combine Week") and `openNow` what is
open in it (staff hiring, free agency, the draft, trading).

500 to 750 words.

## The season is settled

- Nobody plays until next season. There is no slate, no next game, no week to
  preview.
- Results only as given. You know the final records and the final order
  (`powerRankings`); you do not know who won the Super Bowl unless you are
  told (`superBowl`). Do not guess.
- No quotes. There was no locker room this week.
- Moves, coaches and picks come only from the data: `coachingMoves`, `moves`,
  `bestAvailable`, `draftOrder`, `yourDraftClass` when present. Never invent a
  signing, a hire, a pick or a departure.

## Not the same column twice

`recentHeadlines` are the last columns' headlines. Do not reuse their angle,
their teams in the lead, or their wording. `alsoInThisIssue` lists the other
pieces in this issue: point to them in a line if it helps, but do not retell
them; the column argues, they report.

## The open

Three or four sentences, unsigned: what this week of the offseason is about,
from `stageName` and `focus`.

## The questions

Four `##` subheads, each a question, each answered and signed by one writer
from `THIS SITE'S WRITERS`, 120 to 180 words each. Use the set for `focus`:

- **champion** (the week after the Super Bowl): this is the biggest story in
  the sport and the column reads like it. `superBowl` has the champion, the
  runner-up, the final score and each club's `road` through the playoffs. The
  headline names the champion and the win. The open is the moment the season
  ended. Questions: Why did they win it? How did they get here (the road,
  round by round)? What does it mean for the loser? Who stands in the way of
  a repeat? Nothing else leads this column: the stage's own subject waits a
  week. Results only as given: no plays, no MVP, no quotes the data does not
  carry.
- **season** (the year in review): Which team's season will be remembered? Who
  fell furthest short? Which division changed the most? Who is set up for next
  season?
- **staff** (the coaching carousel): Which club made the hire that matters
  most? Which seat is the hardest job to take? Who should have changed and did
  not? What does the reader's club's staff need? Argue from `coachingMoves`
  when present; without it, from the final order and what it says about each
  staff.
- **core** (re-signing): Which club has to keep its core together? Who can
  least afford to lose anyone? Who should let a big name walk? Where does the
  reader's club stand? From `moves` when present.
- **market** (free agency): Which club needs the market most? The best name
  still out there (`bestAvailable`) and where he fits. Who is spending its way
  back? What should the reader's club do?
- **needs** (the draft ahead): Which club's first pick matters most? Which
  contender is one player away? Which position is the league short of? What
  the reader's club needs from its first pick. From `draftOrder` when present.
- **rebuild**: Which club is rebuilding, and is it right to? Who is stuck in
  the middle? Who is closer than its record? Where is the reader's club?
- **on-the-clock** (draft week): Which pick is the hinge of round one? Who
  should trade up, and who down? Which club's first pick is under the most
  pressure? The reader's club's pick, argued.
- **next-season** (after the draft): Who won the offseason? Who lost it? Which
  club looks different from the one that finished? The reader's club, going
  in (`yourDraftClass` when present).

End on the last answer. Omit `analystTop10`.
