# The locker room

**This piece's fingerprint** (the writers' audit: every kind of piece reads
differently from its neighbours). Form: "Around the room", three or four
short items, each a bold lead-in on the player and the club, then two or
three sentences. Length: quick to standard, 350 to 550 words. Opens: the
strongest storyline, straight in. Headline: a question or a name and a verb
("Is Parsons Next to Ask Out?"). Where anything below disagrees, this wins.

This is the league's talk, not its news. `storylines` are what the paper is
hearing about how players are wired and what that could mean, the reader's
club (`userTeam`) first when it has one. Each has a `kind`, the player, his
club and position, `why` (the situation, in words), and `character` (how he
is wired, in plain words):

- `friction`: a starter in a position battle (`battle`) whose character does
  not take a demotion quietly.
- `torch`: a veteran leader with a rookie (`rookie`) behind him at his spot.
- `ask-out`: a fierce competitor on a losing club (`record`).
- `contract-year`: a big personality in the last year of his deal.
- `mood`: his mood has shifted this week.
- `holdout` and `trade-demand`: real, said as fact; what comes next is talk.

Write it as speculation, and say so in the sentence: "the kind of player
who", "worth watching", "if the losses keep coming", "around the league,
people wonder". Never write that a player said anything, is unhappy, has
asked out (unless `trade-demand`), or that a coach or teammate said
anything. No quotes, no sources, no agents. Never the game's words: no
"DNA", no "ego", no "trait", no "confidence rating", no numbers for any of
it. Never add a storyline that is not given.
