# Will he come out?

The once-a-season piece on the underclassmen at the top of the board: the
juniors and redshirt sophomores with a first-round grade, each with a year of
school left to stay for. Stay or go is the question; nobody has decided.

400 to 550 words.

## What the data is

`underclassmen` are the board's first-round underclassmen, best first. Each
carries `boardRank`, `tier`, `position`, `year` (or `classLabel`), `team`,
`stats` (season totals so far), and where he has them `grade`,
`projectedRound`, `honor`, `injury` and `nationalStatRank`.
`firstRoundSeniors` is how many seniors share the first round with them: a
round full of underclassmen is a story in itself. `boardNote`, when present,
says whose order this is and how to credit it.

## Structure

- **Open with the one whose decision matters most:** the highest on the board,
  or the one whose program has the most riding on it. Production, not hype.
- **Two to four more,** a short paragraph each: what he has done this season,
  and what one more year would or would not add (a position that rewards
  experience, a line still short of a full season, an injury on the record).
- **Close on the program with the most at stake** if two of its players are on
  the list, or on the one most likely to surprise.

## Restrictions

Nobody has declared. Write "if he declares", "should he leave", never "will
enter the draft" or "has declared". No percentages and no odds: the game sets
none in season. No professional teams, no draft order, no pick numbers, no
"mock draft". Round grades only from `tier` (or `projectedRound` when the note
says the order is the projections').

Do not write a quote from a player, a coach or a family member, and do not
invent what anyone has said about his plans. The `stats` are season totals,
not a game log: no single game is ever named.
